This page explores the ideas, concepts, and design decisions that shaped the weapon's appearance, movement, and identity into what it is today.

[ 01 ] Visual Foundation

The original concept established the direction for Phantom's Sorrow and introduced the spectral identity the weapon still carries today. It never quite felt finished, so instead of continuing to tweak it, the model was rebuilt from the ground up into the version seen today.

✦

[ 02 ] Animation Exploration

A variety of animation concepts were explored during the early design process to better define how Phantom's Sorrow should feel in motion. The original ideas ranged from a planted/leaning idle pose and ground-dragging movement to a segmented blocking animation that surrounded the wielder. Some concepts were refined, others were set aside, but each played a part in shaping the weapon's final identity and the way it conveys weight, momentum, and an almost unnatural presence.

[ 03 ] Spectral Identity

More than just its appearance, Phantom's Sorrow was designed around a single idea: a weapon with a will of its own. Every concept explored throughout development was created to reinforce that feeling. The spectral flames, transparency effects, segmented parts, and visual effects were all designed to support the same identity, making the blade feel as though a lingering spirit was always present.

The goal was to create a blade that didn't simply follow its wielder, but instead felt as though it had intentions of its own. Every concept was designed to reinforce that feeling, making Phantom's Sorrow feel less like an object and more like a presence.

✦

[ 04 ] Origins

The idea behind Phantom's Sorrow began years before the weapon had an official design in mind. Early designs were built around the idea of a segmented blade and were inspired by weapons and mechanics that stood out compared to others. Although those early ideas never became a finished project, they laid the foundations for what eventually became Phantom's Sorrow, proving that even unfinished concepts can grow into something entirely their own.
